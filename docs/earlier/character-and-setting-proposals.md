# Loop puzzle: four character and setting proposals

Nov 3, 2026 · @Robert

Four settings, each built around one non-human character with human traits, and each picking a different material to ride the belt: sand, water, jelly, and light. All four keep the same core loop (material circles on an outer belt, the player drops it into the rows where it belongs, a small holding area is the space that makes it hard), so the choice is about feel, look, and story rather than rules.

Each proposal covers the same eight points: the character, the world and premise, the material and how it feels, how the mechanic maps onto the world, what the finished picture looks like, palette, how levels are grouped, and the main risk. Every one is checked against the six pillars, with no timers, no randomness, and nothing that needs a hint button to make sense.

## 1. Pebble and the sand paintings (sand)

Pebble is a palm-sized clay figure who wakes up in a sleepy desert town where the wind has blown every sand painting blank, and decides to make them bright again.

- **Character:** a round little clay person with a thumbprint still pressed into its back from whoever shaped it. Human traits: hums while it works, wipes its brow, proudly steps back to admire each finished painting. Slightly clumsy, never discouraged.
- **World and premise:** a quiet oasis town of courtyards, rooftops and market stalls. Each level is one faded sand painting on a wall, floor or tile. Townsfolk are never shown in full; you see their doors open and lanterns light as their painting returns.
- **Material and feel:** fine colored sand. It hisses softly as it pours, piles up in little dunes, and settles flat with a satisfying shimmer. Strong ASMR potential, and the pouring is easy to read at a glance.
- **Mechanic mapping:** the belt is a creaking wooden conveyor turned by a small windmill. Rows are carved grooves in the painting. The holding space is a handful of clay bowls Pebble can set aside. A dead end shows as the windmill slowing to a stop and Pebble sitting down with a puzzled shrug.
- **The finished picture:** flat, grainy mosaic shapes, close to Art Puzzle's chunky cut-out look. Sand texture gives every color a soft, matte edge.
- **Palette:** terracotta, ochre, sage, dusty rose, faded indigo. Warm and earthy, nothing neon.
- **Level grouping:** chapters are neighborhoods of the town (the well, the market, the rooftops, the old temple). Finishing a chapter rebuilds that part of a town map you can browse.
- **Main risk:** a desert can read as dry or empty. The fix is to keep it lush around the oasis, with palms, water and evening light.

## 2. Nimbo and the washed-out postcards (water)

Nimbo is a small raincloud who drifts over the world collecting postcards that the rain has washed pale, and refills each one with tinted rainwater.

- **Character:** a puffy cloud with stubby arms, rosy cheeks and a tiny satchel for postcards. Human traits: gets excited and puffs up, sniffles a drizzle when something goes wrong, waves at birds. Curious and a bit of a daydreamer.
- **World and premise:** each chapter is a place Nimbo floats over (a fishing harbor, tulip fields, a mountain village, a night city). Each level is one postcard from that place. As cards are restored, they pin up on a travel board in Nimbo's attic.
- **Material and feel:** tinted water, like watercolor. It pours with a gentle trickle, ripples as rows fill, and the color soaks outward into the paper. Calm, wet, rain-on-the-roof sound design.
- **Mechanic mapping:** the belt is a rain gutter or little aqueduct running around the postcard frame, with droplets carried in small cups. Rows are channels in the paper. The holding space is a few puddles or teacups on the frame. A dead end shows as the gutter going still and Nimbo looking up at a grey sky.
- **The finished picture:** soft watercolor regions with slightly bled edges, framed as a postcard with a stamp and a short handwritten note.
- **Palette:** washed blues, seafoam, lilac, buttercream, coral. Airy and low-saturation.
- **Level grouping:** chapters are destinations, so the collection itself becomes the progression. Natural room for seasonal postcard sets later.
- **Main risk:** liquid is harder to read as distinct units than sand or jelly. The fix is to carry it in clear cups on the belt so each unit stays countable.

## 3. Gumdrop's jelly bakery (jelly)

Gumdrop is a small gummy who runs a corner sweet shop and makes jelly window cakes, clear slabs with pictures set inside them in colored jelly.

- **Character:** a translucent, bean-shaped gummy with a baker's hat that is too big for it. Human traits: wobbles when it laughs, taste-tests everything, writes little name cards for each cake. Warm, proud of its work, a bit of a perfectionist.
- **World and premise:** one cozy shop that grows over the game. Each level is a customer's order, a cake with a picture inside (a cat, a sunset, a bouquet). Finished cakes go into the shop window, and new regulars start dropping by.
- **Material and feel:** soft colored jelly. It plops into rows with a squish, jiggles, then sets glossy. Very tactile on a phone, and the wobble makes every drop feel like a reward.
- **Mechanic mapping:** the belt is a candy-factory conveyor circling the mold, carrying jelly cubes. Rows are sections of the mold. The holding space is a few small ramekins on the counter. A dead end shows as the conveyor stopping and Gumdrop scratching its head over the recipe.
- **The finished picture:** clean, glossy shapes with light passing through, like stained glass made of jelly. Easy to keep the chunky Art Puzzle look.
- **Palette:** bright, juicy candy colors: cherry red, tangerine, lemon, lime, raspberry pink, grape, blueberry. Saturated and cheerful, but with a soft gloss and clean white highlights instead of pure neon, so it pops without screaming. The shop around the board stays warm cream and wood so the jelly is the brightest thing on screen.
- **Level grouping:** chapters are menu seasons (spring fruits, summer sodas, autumn spice, winter festival). The shop interior upgrades as you go, which gives a soft meta-progression without new rules.
- **Main risk:** sweets can tip into a kids-only look. The fix is a grown-up, café-like art direction for the shop and characters, letting the jelly carry the bright color.

## 4. Glim and the dark windows (light)

Glim is a young firefly who lives in a hilltop town where the stained-glass windows have gone dark, and gathers colored glow to light them again, one window per night.

- **Character:** a chubby firefly with a lantern belly, big eyes and a knitted scarf. Human traits: yawns at dawn, cheers when a window lights, keeps a little notebook of every window it has fixed. Brave but a bit shy, the kind of hero who is surprised to be one.
- **World and premise:** a storybook town at dusk (the bakery, the library, the clock tower, the chapel). Each level is one window. As windows light, the town slowly glows across a night map, which makes progress visible at a distance.
- **Material and feel:** glowing colored motes, like liquid light or luminous pollen. They pour in soft streams, brighten a row as it fills, and give a gentle chime when it completes. The most magical of the four, and the board literally gets lighter as you tidy it.
- **Mechanic mapping:** the belt is a stream of light circling the window frame, carried in tiny glass lanterns. Rows are the lead-lined panes. The holding space is a few spare jars on the windowsill. A dead end shows as the lanterns dimming and Glim's belly flickering low.
- **The finished picture:** stained-glass panels with dark lead lines, which naturally gives clean outlines in the Art Puzzle spirit.
- **Palette:** deep twilight blue background with warm amber, rose, jade and soft gold glow. Colors look rich against the dark without shouting.
- **Level grouping:** chapters are nights in the town, each lighting a street. Finishing a chapter could bring morning, with the windows seen in daylight.
- **Main risk:** glow on dark backgrounds can lose contrast between similar colors. The fix is to add a shape or pattern per color for readability and color-blind support.

## Side by side

All four fit the pillars; they differ mostly in how the material feels and what you collect along the way.

| Proposal | Material | Pour feel | Picture style | What you collect | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pebble | Sand | Soft hiss, piles and settles | Grainy matte mosaic | Rebuilt town neighborhoods | Desert can feel dry |
| Nimbo | Water | Trickle, ripple, soaks in | Watercolor postcards | Postcards on a travel board | Liquid is harder to count |
| Gumdrop | Jelly | Plop, jiggle, sets glossy | Translucent jelly cakes | A growing shop window | Can look too childish |
| Glim | Light | Glowing stream, chime | Stained-glass panes | A town lighting up at night | Contrast on dark backgrounds |

A few questions that usually settle it:

- Which pour would you most like to watch a thousand times?
- Do you want the game to feel like daytime (Pebble, Nimbo, Gumdrop) or evening (Glim)?
- Should the collection be places you visit (Nimbo), one place you restore (Pebble, Glim), or one place that grows (Gumdrop)?

Mixing is fine too, for example Glim's character in Pebble's town, or Nimbo's postcards with jelly instead of water.
